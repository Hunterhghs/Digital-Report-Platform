Methodology & caveats

What “survivability risk” means here

A 0–100 index of the likelihood that a country’s population faces severe, systemic threats to life, health and livelihoods this century — not a prediction of collapse. It combines six interacting stresses of the polycrisis: climate hazard exposure, pollution burden, conflict & violence, inequality & poverty, food & water insecurity, and the deficit in adaptive capacity (state fragility, institutions, readiness).

Baseline (c. 2025)

Each dimension is a rounded, composite estimate informed by public indices: ND-GAIN (exposure & readiness), INFORM Risk, WHO / AQLI ambient PM2.5, IHME Global Burden of Disease, UCDP / ACLED and the Global Peace Index, World Bank Poverty & Inequality Platform, FAO SOFI / IPC food-insecurity data, WRI Aqueduct water stress, and the Fragile States Index. Population figures approximate the UN World Population Prospects 2024 (medium variant).

Projection model

  • Warming follows IPCC AR6 median global surface temperature for each SSP.
  • Climate hazard saturates toward 100 with additional warming, scaled by a country sensitivity factor (tropics, drylands and low-lying coasts are more sensitive).
  • Adaptive capacity, pollution and inequality evolve with each SSP’s socio-economic narrative; warming adds wildfire/ozone pollution and climate-driven inequality.
  • Food & water rise with warming, damped where adaptive capacity is strong.
  • Conflict relaxes from today’s level toward a structural level driven by capacity, inequality, food stress and climate.
  • Composite = weighted mean of the six dimensions, amplified up to 15% when several stresses are simultaneously ≥ 60 (compounding risk).

Read with care

This is an exploratory scenario tool with deliberately simple, transparent dynamics. Scores are indicative and rounded; they are not official statistics or forecasts. Small states, sub-national hot spots, tipping points, migration, pandemics, technological change and policy surprises are not modelled. Use the weights to test how sensitive rankings are to what you value, and consult the source datasets for decisions.
